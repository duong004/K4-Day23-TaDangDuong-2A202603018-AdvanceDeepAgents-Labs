# Survey about LLM Agents and Tool Use

## TL;DR
- LLM agents utilize complex architectural methodologies that integrate tool use effectively, enhancing their operational capabilities [1][2].
- Empirical benchmarks reveal significant performance discrepancies, prompting the need for robust evaluation frameworks [3][4].
- Recent trends highlight ongoing challenges such as adversarial vulnerabilities and integration consistency across platforms [5].

## Background
The adoption of LLM (Large Language Models) as agents has transformed computational tasks across various domains. These agents leverage natural language processing and machine learning technologies to perform tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously. Understanding the foundational concepts and architectural designs surrounding LLM agents is critical for advancing their capabilities and resolving challenges associated with their deployment [6][7].

## Core Architectural Methodologies and Variations
A variety of architectural frameworks such as FALAA and other modular frameworks enable comprehensive agent capabilities. These methodologies separate the architecture's functions into modular components, providing flexibility in tool integration and ensuring that agents can operate efficiently in dynamic environments. Studies have demonstrated that such designs enhance cooperative behaviors and optimize overall tool use efficacy [2][8].

## Empirical Benchmarks and Real-World Applications
Recent research has emphasized the importance of empirical evaluations for LLM agents. Benchmarks demonstrate substantial differences in performance metrics, which highlight the necessity for robust evaluation frameworks for different implementations, thereby influencing their application in real-world scenarios. For instance, evaluations in dynamic settings reveal challenges regarding robustness and reliability in task completion [3][9].

## Trends and Open Problems
In the last two years, there has been a significant emphasis on the autonomy of LLM agents. Open challenges include the need for improved oversight mechanisms in performance evaluations. Furthermore, researchers debate the integration of these agents across various platforms while striving for performance consistency, prompting inquiries into best practices for their deployment [10][11].
